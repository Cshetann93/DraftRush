The Dilemma

Always confused by too many ideas?

When you try to fit every thought into page one, your narrative loses flow, pacing, and focus.

PHASE 01: THE BASELINE
02/11
Step One

Start With The Context

Lay Down The Plain Facts

Do not jump straight into complex plot twists.

Establish the baseline world immediately. What is standard normal life for your character before everything goes off the rails?

DRAFTSRUSH STUDIO draftsrush.com
PHASE 02: FRICTION
03/11
Step Two

Frame The Core Problem

What Broke?

A story does not start until something breaks down.

Isolate one distinct problem. If your protagonist has five unrelated life crises in chapter one, your audience has zero reasons to care about any of them.

DRAFTSRUSH STUDIO draftsrush.com
PHASE 03: THE CLOCK
04/11
Step Three

Introduce The Urgency

Why Now?

Why must this issue be solved today instead of next year?

Add a ticking clock or a rising cost of doing nothing. If walking away costs nothing, you do not have a story.

PHASE 04: THE CATALYST
05/11
Step Four

Reveal The Decision

No Turning Back

Your character chooses a specific path to tackle the problem.

This is the big thesis of your script. It must be unique, high-stakes, and completely cut off their old way of living.

DRAFTSRUSH STUDIO draftsrush.com
PHASE 05: THE STAKES
06/11
Step Five

Define The Value

What Is Won Or Lost?

State the exact payoff of success.

If they pull this off, what does their life look like? Keep the target concrete so your audience can cheer for a clear finish line.

PHASE 06: EDITING IDEAS
07/11
Step Six

Pick 3 Major Steps

Say No To The Rest

This is where most writers get stuck.

Filter out your 20 random ideas. Select only 3 crucial actions your protagonist must complete to hit their goal. Discard everything else.

DRAFTSRUSH STUDIO draftsrush.com
PHASE 07: VERIFICATION
08/11
Step Seven

Add Logical Proof

No Easy Miracles

Every action step must earn its solution.

Do not let characters magically resolve obstacles. Force them to sacrifice, negotiate, or use real technical skills to move forward.

DRAFTSRUSH STUDIO draftsrush.com
PHASE 08: THE BLUEPRINT
09/11
Step Eight

Build The Sequence

Order Your Actions

Arrange scenes so that each one causes the next.

Replace 'and then' with 'therefore' or 'but'. When action A fails, action B becomes mandatory. That is how real pacing works.

PHASE 09: EXECUTION
10/11
Step Nine

Close Out & Publish

Stop Rewriting Draft 1

Finish the blueprint, write page one to the end, and ship it.

Perfectionism is just procrastination. A finished 1,000-word draft teaches you more than three years of untouched brainstorms.
