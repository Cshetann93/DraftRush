RULE 01 • SPATIAL CONTINUITY
02 / 08
🔄 THE 180° DEGREE RULE
AXIS OF ACTION 180° SAFE ARC ✅
🎥 CAM A ❌ CROSSED 🎥 CAM B
📌 BEST USE CASE

Two characters facing each other in dialogue or confrontation.

🧠 ON-SCREEN PSYCHOLOGY

Keeping cameras on one side preserves eyeline continuity. Crossing the axis tricks the viewer's brain into thinking actors suddenly turned their backs on each other.

✍️ HOW TO SCRIPT IT (AV)

Label coverage in the left column as: 'Cam A (Host Profile) / Cam B (Guest Match)' so editors never jump the line.

RULE 02 • POWER DYNAMICS
03 / 08
📐 LOW ANGLE VS HIGH ANGLE
LOW ANGLE = POWER 👑 HIGH ANGLE = WEAK 😰
📌 BEST USE CASE

Confrontations, negotiations, intimidating villains, or cornered victims.

🧠 ON-SCREEN PSYCHOLOGY

Shooting from below physically magnifies actor stature against the ceiling or sky. Shooting from above looks down on the subject, making them appear helpless.

✍️ HOW TO SCRIPT IT (AV)

Note '[LOW ANGLE - 35mm PUSH]' during moments of triumph, or '[HIGH ANGLE - CRANE DOWN]' to emphasize defeat.

RULE 03 • PSYCHOLOGICAL UNREST
04 / 08
🌪️ THE DUTCH ANGLE (CANTED TILT)
⟳ TILTED -20° OFF HORIZON INSTINCTIVE PANIC ⚠️
📌 BEST USE CASE

Psychological spirals, character deceit, horror reveals, or intoxication.

🧠 ON-SCREEN PSYCHOLOGY

Humans balance the world against a level horizon. Throwing that horizon off by 15° to 30° strips away subconscious comfort, putting the audience on high alert.

✍️ HOW TO SCRIPT IT (AV)

Add '[DUTCH ROLL / CANTED HORIZON]' right before a major reveal or when a scene's reality is questioned.

RULE 04 • COMPOSITIONAL BALANCE
05 / 08
🔲 RULE OF THIRDS & EYE-LINE
🎯 EYE-LINE LOCKED ON UPPER HORIZONTAL THIRD
📌 BEST USE CASE

Standard talking heads, cinematic interviews, and documentary setups.

🧠 ON-SCREEN PSYCHOLOGY

Centering a subject creates formal, rigid symmetry. Placing eyes along the top third creates natural viewing comfort and opens negative space for on-screen context.

✍️ HOW TO SCRIPT IT (AV)

Note '[FRAMED STAGE LEFT - THIRDS]' so your editor has clean open negative space to display chart pop-ups or visual assets.

RULE 05 • ACTION DIRECTION
06 / 08
🏃 LEAD ROOM & LOOKING SPACE
LEAD ROOM (OPEN DIRECTION) ➔
AVOID: NOSE TO EDGE 🛑
📌 BEST USE CASE

Tracking moving subjects, driving scenes, or characters searching a room.

🧠 ON-SCREEN PSYCHOLOGY

Audiences need to see where a subject is headed. Crowding an actor's face against the edge of frame creates claustrophobia and suffocated momentum.

✍️ HOW TO SCRIPT IT (AV)

Write '[TRACKING SHOT - GENEROUS LEAD ROOM]' for confident momentum, or '[ZERO LEAD ROOM]' when trapping a character.

RULE 06 • INTIMACY & PROXIMITY
07 / 08
🗣️ THE DIRTY OVER-THE-SHOULDER
FOREGROUND SILHOUETTE FOCUSED SUBJECT IN DEPTH
📌 BEST USE CASE

Intense one-on-one arguments, intimate romantic beats, or interrogations.

🧠 ON-SCREEN PSYCHOLOGY

Including a slice of the listener's shoulder anchors both actors in one shared three-dimensional physical room, pulling the audience directly into the emotional tension.

✍️ HOW TO SCRIPT IT (AV)

Note '[DIRTY OTS - TIGHT ON EYES]' to escalate conversational heat without cutting away to isolated singles.
