CORE CONCEPT

Find a Small World

ONE ARENA, ZERO SPRAWL

A short film cannot support five subplots, ten locations, or four flashbacks.

Pick one contained arena: a late-night diner booth, an interrogation desk, or a stalled elevator. Spatial restraint forces pure character tension.

SCENE TIMING

Enter Late, Leave Early

CUT THE WARM-UP

Never show characters waking up, parking cars, or greeting each other.

Open the scene at the final second before a decision happens. Cut away the instant the consequence lands. Every page must fight for its runtime.

CHARACTER GOAL

Isolate a Single Want

ONE OBJECTIVE

Your protagonist needs one concrete target within the first 60 seconds.

Not "finding inner peace," but unlocking a safe, reaching a dying phone, or concealing evidence. Make the physical finish line unmistakable.

PRODUCTION REALITY

Write for Real Assets

AUDIT BEFORE WRITING

Do not write exploding cars or neon cityscapes on an indie budget.

Catalog assets you control: a tool shed, a basement storage room, or two talented actors. Turn actual physical limitations into deliberate cinematic style.

RULE 05: SHOW VS TELL
06/12
CINEMATIC WEIGHT

Cut 50% of Dialogue

ACTIONS OVER SPEECHES

Beginner scripts explain emotion through conversation.

Cinema communicates through physical behavior: trembling hands, sliding a deadbolt, or breaking eye contact. If action proves it, delete the dialogue line.

VISUAL BEHAVIOR: 75% SPOKEN DIALOGUE: 25%

Avoid Gimmick Endings

INEVITABILITY

"It was all a dream" or sudden unearned twists cheat your audience.

The climax must feel surprising yet inevitable. The resolution has to come directly from your protagonist's decisions, not convenient chance.
